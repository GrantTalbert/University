(e) The solution with the initial value problem $(u_0,v_0)=(1,1)$ is an equilibrium solution. Any solution of the IVP $(u_0,v_0)=(\alpha ,\alpha )$ for some $\alpha \in\mathbb{R}\setminus\left\{ 1 \right\} $ will accelerate along the line $v=u$. If $\alpha >1$, then the solution will accelerate in the positive direction, and if $\alpha <1$, then it will accelerate in the negative direction. If $(u_0,v_0)=(\alpha ,1 )$, then $v$ will remain $1$ for all $t$, and $u$ will accelerate to $+\infty$ if $\alpha >1$, and $u\to -\infty$ if $\alpha <1$. Similarly, for $(u_0,v_0)=(1,\alpha )$, the same behavior is observed, except $u$ is fixed and $v$ accelerates. For all other solutions, $u$ and $v$ will both accelerate to positive or negative $\infty$, specifically positive if they're greater than 1, and negative if they're less than 1. They will approach one of the straight line solutions we described previously, since they will be growing faster in one directiton than the other, but will never actually reach the straight line solution due to Picard's theorem.

\section*{Supplimental 3}
In the continuous model, $y=Ce^{-\lambda t }$. Note that $y(0)=C$, so $C$ is the initial amount of radioactive materials, which is analogous to $z_0$. We then can express
\[
	Ce^{-\lambda t}= Cr^{t}
,\]
where $z_n$ is the number of radioactive atoms after $n$ days. In other words, $n=t$. We then have the following relationship between $r$ and $\lambda $:
\[
	e^{-\lambda }=r
.\]
To find the half life, set
\[
	\frac{C}{2}=Cr^t
.\]
It follows that
\begin{align*}
	&\qquad\;\;\;\frac{C}{2}=Cr^t\\
	&\implies \frac{1}{2}=r^t\\
	&\implies \ln \frac{1}{2}=t\ln r\\
	&\implies t=\frac{\ln \frac{1}{2}}{\ln r} 
.\end{align*}
