\lecture{10}{Wed 23 Oct 2024 12:21}{Inhomogeneous Continuation}
Linearity principles hold for inhomogeneous systems. In the case
\[
	\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Y}}{\mathrm{d}t} =A \mathbf{Y}+\mathbf{G}(t)
,\]
Let $\mathbf{Y}=(0,e^t)$.
the general solution to the homogeneous case plus a particular solution to the inhomogeneous case will give the general solution for the inhomogeneous case. We can sometimes guess $\mathbf{Y}=Ae^{\lambda t}\mathbf{v}$ for some vector $\mathbf{v}$ and constants $A,\lambda $, but there is also a more analytic method.

Du Hamel's formula says the following. Let
	 \[
		\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Y}}{\mathrm{d}t} =A \mathbf{Y}+\mathbf{G}(t)
	.\]
Rearrange it to give
\[
	\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Y}}{\mathrm{d}t} -A \mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{G}(t)
.\]
Choose $M(t)$ some matrix such that
\[
	M(t)\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Y}}{\mathrm{d}t} -M(t)A \mathbf{Y}=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t} M(t) \mathbf{Y}
.\]
It follows that
\[
	M(t)=e^{-At}
.\]
Thus, let
\[
	e^{-At}\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Y}}{\mathrm{d}t}-e^{-At}A \mathbf{Y}=e^{-At}\mathbf{G}(t)
.\]
By the inverse of the product rule,
\[
	\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t} \left( e^{-At}\mathbf{Y} \right) =e^{-At}\mathbf{G}(t)
.\]
Integrating, we find
\[
	\mathbf{Y}(t)=e^{At}\int e^{-At}\mathbf{G}(t) \,\mathrm{d} t
,\]
since the inverse of the matrix $e^{-At}$ is $e^{At}$. Another simple way of stating this is
\[
	\mathbf{Y}(t)=e^{tA}\mathbf{Y}(0)+e^{tA}\int_{0}^{t} e^{-tA}\mathbf{G}(t) \,\mathrm{d} t
.\]
