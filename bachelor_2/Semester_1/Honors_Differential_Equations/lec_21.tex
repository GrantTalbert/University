\lecture{21}{Wed 20 Nov 2024 13:03}{Hamiltonian Differential Equations}
Consider the mass on a spring, with position $x$ governed by
\[
	m \frac{\mathrm{d}^2x}{\mathrm{d}t^2} =-kx
.\]
Note that in this case, we are ignoring friction. In physics, we talk a lot about energy. Specifically, the kinetic energy of a particle, or the potential evergy of a particle. A conservative force, $\mathbf{F}=-\nabla f$ for some potential function $f$, conserves energy. For example, the value $V=\frac{1}{2}kx^2$ is the potential function for the restorative force of a spring $F=kx$. The total energy $H(x,v)$, where $v=\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t} $ is velocity, is
\[
	H(x,v)=\frac{1}{2}mv^2+\frac{1}{2}kx^2
.\]
The first part is our general kinetic energy formula, and the second is our $V$. This quantity is called the \textbf{Hamiltonian}, and systems of this type are called Hamiltonian systems.
