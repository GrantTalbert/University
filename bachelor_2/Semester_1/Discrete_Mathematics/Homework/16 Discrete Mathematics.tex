\section{Content Reflection}
I'm not sure what to say about the Concept Learning Marker for a number of reasons. We were never given a definition of what constitutes a \emph{combinatorial} game, and although I have a vague idea of what combinatorics is, I wouldn't say it's nearly good enough to classify a game as being combinatorial in nature. Although we have played a few tile games and variations thereof, and although there are various connections between them, this only implies that a combinatorial game in the general sense must share at least some of these properties, and it's not readily apparent which properties those would be.

Without a working knowledge of what specifically a combinatorial game entails, it's difficult to give a precise response to the questions relevant to this section. However, assuming a combinatorial game in the general sense is similar enough to the tile games we have explored as to be [interchangable], it's still not obvious what to say here. The method I have used to explore these combinatorial games generally entails three steps:
\begin{enumerate}
	\item Simplify the game to a simpler problem, and solve it numerically. Look for important properties. Solve the game for slightly more complex situations, and track these properties to identify any emergent patterns that determine a solution.
	\item Extrapolate the patterns, and look for any intuition as to why they might hold in general.
	\item Formalize the game mathematically and prove the patterns hold in general. This pattern can now be used to systematically solve the game.
\end{enumerate}
