\section*{Problem 1}
Let $r$ be a root of $f(x)$ in $\mathbb{C} $, viewed as an extension field of $\mathbb{Q} $, and note that $r\neq 0$:
\[
	0^4+0^3-0^2-0+1=1\neq 0
.\]
Therefore, since $\mathbb{C} $ is a field, $-1/r$ exists. We have
\begin{align*}
	\left( -\frac{1}{r}  \right) ^4+\left( -\frac{1}{r}  \right) ^3-\left( -\frac{1}{r}  \right) ^2-\left( -\frac{1}{r}  \right) +1&=\frac{1}{r^4} -\frac{1}{r^3} -\frac{1}{r^2} +\frac{1}{r} +1\\
																       &=\frac{1}{r^4} -\frac{r}{r^4} -\frac{r^2}{r^4} +\frac{r^3}{r^4} +\frac{r^4}{r^4} \\
																       &=\frac{1-r-r^2+r^3+r^4}{r^4} \\
																       &=\frac{r^4+r^3-r^2-r+1}{r^4} \\
																       &=\frac{f(r)}{r^4} \\
																       &=\frac{0}{r^4} \\
																       &=0
.\end{align*}
Therefore, $-1/r$ is a root of $f$.
