\lecture{16}{Thu 03 Apr 2025 11:00}{Homotopy}
We can define a functor $\mathbf{Top} \to \mathbf{Grp} $, and the image of a topological space $X$ under the functor is called the first fundamental group $\pi _1(X)$.
\begin{definition}[Homotopic]\label{dfn:6.1}
	Two paths $\phi _1,\phi _2$ from $x$ to $y$ are homotopic if there exists a continuous map $\Phi : [0,1]\times [0,1] \to X$ such that  $\Phi (0,-)=\phi _1$, and $\Phi (1,-)=\phi _2$. We call $\Phi $ a homotopy.
\end{definition}

Clearly, homotopy is an equivalence relation. We would think there would be a way to identify homotopic paths with each other functorially.
