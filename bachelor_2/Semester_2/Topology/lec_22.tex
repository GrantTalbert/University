\begin{definition}[Free Product]\label{dfn:wj}
	Let $\left\{ G_{\alpha }  \right\}_{\alpha \in A} $ be a collection of groups. The \emph{free product }of these groups is tbe group generated by all words in $\left\{ G_{\alpha }  \right\} _{\alpha \in A} $, under the operation of concatenation, where word reduction is inherited from the group structure.
\end{definition}

The free product $G_1*G_2$ is the coproduct of groups. We can use the free product to compute fundamental groups for topological spaces that have been ``glued together''. Consider
\[
	X=\bigcup_{\alpha \in A} X_\alpha 
,\]
with
\[
	\bigcap_{\alpha \in A} X_\alpha =x_0
.\]
Also assume $X$ is path connected. The collection of spaces is joined at a point. We can compute $\pi _1(X,x_0)$. Since each $X_\alpha $ has an inclusion into $X$, by the universal property of free products there is a group homomorphism
\[
	\Phi : \coprod_{\alpha \in A} \pi _1(X_\alpha ,x_0)\to \pi _1(X,x_0)
.\]
By the first isomorphism theorem, we have
\[
	\left(\coprod_{\alpha \in A}\pi _1(X_\alpha ,x_0)\right) / \ker \Phi \cong \pi _1(X,x_0)
.\]
The map is surjetive,a nd the kernel is something.

Let $X^0$ be a discrete set of points. We call these 0-cells. Inductively we construct $X^n$ from $X^{n-1} $ by attaching $n$-cells $D_\alpha ^n$ via maps
\[
	S_\alpha ^{n-1} \to X^{n-1} ,
\]
where $S_\alpha ^{n-1} \cong \partial D_\alpha ^{n-1} $.  We then define
\[
	X^n = \left( X^{n-1} \amalg \coprod_{\alpha \in A}D_\alpha ^{n}  \right) /\sim ,
\]
for $x\sim \phi_\alpha (x)$ with $x\in \partial D_\alpha ^{n} $ and $\phi_\alpha (x)\in X^{n-1} $. Then $X^n$ is a topological space. These are \emph{finite CW complexes}.

For example, given a 2-disk $D^2$, we can choose a point $\left\{ * \right\} =X^0$ as our 0-cell. We then have another disk as our 2-cell, and a map $\phi_1 : \partial D^2=S^1\to X^1$. If we take $\phi _1=\mathrm{id} $, then $X^1=S^1$.
