By definition, $\varnothing ,\mathcal{T} \in\mathbb{R} $. Now let $\left\{ (-\infty ,p) \right\}_{p\in P} \subseteq \mathcal{T} $, and consider the union over this collection. Let $a=\sup  P$, and let $x\in (-\infty ,a)$. We have that $a<\sup P$, and thus by definition, there exists $p\in P$ such that $a<p$. It follows that $x\in (-\infty ,p)$, and thus we have
\[
	x\in (-\infty ,p) \subseteq \bigcup_{p\in P} (-\infty ,p)
.\]
Therefore, $(-\infty ,a)\subseteq \bigcup_{p\in P} (-\infty ,p)$. Now let $x\in \bigcup_{p\in P} (-\infty ,p)$. It follows that there exists $p\in P $ such that $x\in (-\infty ,p)$, and thus $x<p$ for some $p\in P$. Since $p<\sup P=a$, we have that $x<a$, and thus $x\in (-\infty ,a)$. Therefore,
\[
	\bigcup_{p\in P} (-\infty ,p)=(-\infty ,\sup P)
.\]
Since $(-\infty ,\sup P)$ is of the same form as given in the definition of $\mathcal{T} $, we have $(-\infty ,\sup P)\in \mathcal{T} $. Therefore, $\mathcal{T} $ is closed under union.

Now let $P$ be a finite subset of $\mathbb{R} $, and consider the collection $\left\{ (-\infty ,p) \right\}_{p\in P} $. Since $P$ is finite, it must have a least element. Let $a= \min P$ be this least element, and note that for all $p\in P$, $a\leq p$ implies that $(-\infty ,a)\subseteq (-\infty ,p)$. It follows that
\[
	(-\infty ,a)\bigcap_{p\in P} (-\infty ,p)
.\]
However, we also know that due to how intersections work,
\[
	\bigcap_{p\in P} (-\infty ,p) \subseteq (-\infty ,a)
.\]
Therefore, the finite intersection is of the for $(-\infty ,a)$, and thus is an element of $\mathcal{T} $. We have that $\mathcal{T} $ is closed under finite intersection, and is thus a topology on $\mathbb{R} $.

\[
	B(n)\cap B(m)=\left\{ n-1,n,n+1 \right\} \cap \left\{ m-1,m,m+1 \right\}

\[
	\left( \bigcup_{a\in A} Y_a \right ) ^{c}

By the definition of a topology, $\bigcup_{a\in A} Y_a$ is open, so $\left( \bigcup_{a\in A} Y_a \right ) ^{c} $ is closed.

\[
	\bigcup_{i=1} ^k X_i = \bigcup_{i=1} ^k Y_i^{c} =\left( \bigcap_{i=1} ^k Y_i \right ) ^{c} 
.\]
By definition of a topology, since $Y_i$ is open for all $i$, it follows that $\bigcap_{i=1} ^k Y_i$ is open. Therefore, $\left( \bigcap_{i=1} ^k Y_i \right ) ^{c} $ is closed.
