\section{Problem 2}

Let $x^\mu =(x,y,z)$ be the cartesian coordinate system in $\mathbb{R} ^3$, and $(r,\theta ,\phi )$ the spherical coordinate system
\[
	x=r \sin \theta \cos \phi ,\qquad y=r \sin \theta \sin \phi  ,\qquad z=r \cos \theta 
.\]
The metric then takes the form
\[
	\mathrm{d} s^2=\mathrm{d} r^2+r^2 \mathrm{d} \theta ^2+r^2 \sin^2\theta \mathrm{d} \phi ^2
.\]

\begin{problem}
	A particle moves along a curve
	\[
		x^\mu (\lambda )=(x(\lambda ),y(\lambda ),z(\lambda ))=(\cos \lambda ,\sin \lambda ,\lambda )
	.\]
	Express the path of the particle in the $(r,\theta ,\phi )$ coordinate system.
\end{problem}
\noindent\emph{Solution.} This feels too easy and like a trick question. From simple geometric reasoning, we would think that
	\[
		r^2=x^2+y^2+z^2
	,\]
	\[
		\phi  =\arctan \frac{y}{x} ,\qquad \theta =\arctan \frac{\sqrt{x^2+y^2} }{z^2} 
	.\]
	Some care must be taken to ensure the arctangent is taken in the proper quadrant.
