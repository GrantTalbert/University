\begin{exercise}
	Consider the sets
	\begin{itemize}
		\item $\mathfrak{gl}_n(\mathbb{R} )\coloneqq \mathcal{M}_n(\mathbb{R} )$;
	\item $\mathfrak{sl}_n(\mathbb{R} )\coloneqq \left\{ M\in \mathfrak{gl}_n(\mathbb{R} )\mid \Tr M=0 \right\} $;
	\item $\mathfrak{sl}_n(\mathbb{C} )\coloneqq \left\{ M\in \mathfrak{gl}_n(\mathbb{C} )\mid \Tr M=0 \right\} $;
	\item $\mathfrak{so}_n(\mathbb{R} )\coloneqq \left\{ M\in \mathfrak{sl}_n(\mathbb{R} )\mid M+M^t=0 \right\} $;
	\item $\mathfrak{su}(n)\coloneqq \left\{ M\in \mathfrak{sl}_n(\mathbb{C} )\mid M+M^\dagger=0 \right\} $.
	\end{itemize}
	Show these sets are $\mathbb{R} $-vector spaces, and compute their dimension.
\end{exercise}
\begin{solution}
	First, we show $\mathfrak{gl} _n(\mathbb{R} )$ is a $\mathbb{R} $-vector space. We will use the componentwise matrix notation $M\coloneqq [m_{ij} ]$, where $m_{ij} $ are the components of the matrix $M$ and range over $\left\{ 1, \ldots ,n \right\} $. For any $r\in\mathbb{R} $, define $r[m_{ij} ]=[r m_{ij} ] $. Clearly, $\mathfrak{gl} _n(\mathbb{R} )$ is a group, so we will simply show it is a vector space. Note that $1[m_{ij} ]=[m_{ij} ]$,
	\[
		(r+s)[m_{ij} ]=r[m_{ij} ]+s[m_{ij} ]
	,\]
	and
	\[
		(rs)[m_{ij} ]=[rsm_{ij} ]=r[sm_{ij} ]=r\left( s[m_{ij} ] \right)  
	.\]
	Moreover, $r$ is an endomorphism:
	\[
		r\left( [m_{ij} ]+[n_{ij} ] \right) =r[m_{ij} +n_{ij} ]=[r\left( m_{ij} +n_{ij}  \right) ]=[r m_{ij} +rn_{ij} ]=[r m_{ij} ]+[rn_{ij} ]=r[m_{ij} ]+r[n_{ij} ] 
	.\]
	Therefore, $r\mapsto r$ is a homomorphism $\mathbb{R}  \to \End_{\mathsf{Ab} }(\mathfrak{gl}_n(\mathbb{R} )) $, and $\mathfrak{gl} _n(\mathbb{R} )$ is an $\mathbb{R} $-vector space. Finally, we compute the dimension. Obviously, $\mathfrak{gl}_n(\mathbb{R} )\cong \mathbb{R} ^{n^2} $. This is so much effort to prove so I'm not going to.

	$\mathfrak{sl}_n(\mathbb{R} )$ is very difficult, but note that $\mathfrak{so}_n(\mathbb{R} )\subseteq \mathfrak{sl}_n(\mathbb{R} )\subseteq \mathfrak{gl}_n(\mathbb{R} )$,  so maybe that helps.

	I think the basis for $\mathfrak{so}_n(\mathbb{R} )$ is basically one element per item $m_{ij} $ in the upper triangle (not counting main diagonal), put a 1 there, and put a $-1$ at $m_{ji} $. Then $M^t=-M$ for the basis set. I think this is the entire basis.

	For $\mathfrak{so} _n(\mathbb{R} )$, we have $[m_ij]=-[m_ji]$, and thus the diagonal entries are all 0. It follows that we simply have
	\[
		\dim_{\mathbb{R} } \mathfrak{so}_n(\mathbb{R} )=\frac{n^2-n}{2} 
	.\]
	The $n^2$ is the number of entries in the matrix, the $-n$ removes the diagonal elements, and the division by 2 reduces it to only one of the remaining two triangles, since $m_{ij} =-m_{ji} $.
\end{solution}

\begin{exercise}
	Let $V$ be a $k$-vector space. A \emph{Lie Bracket} on $V$ is an operation $\left[ \cdot ,\cdot  \right] : V\times V \to V$ such that for all $u,v,w\in V $ and $a,b\in k$,
	\begin{itemize}
		\item $[au+bv,w]=a[u,w]+b[v,w]$ and $[w,au+bv]=a[w,u]+b[w,v]$;
		\item $[v,v]=0$;
		\item $[[u,v],w]+[[v,w],u]+[[w,u],v]=0$.
	\end{itemize}
	This last axiom is called the \emph{Jacobi identity}. A vector space endowed with a Lie bracket is called a Lie algebra. Define a category of Lie algebras over a given field. Prove the following:
	\begin{itemize}
		\item In a Lie algebra, $[u,v]=-[v,u]$ for all $u,v\in V$.
		\item If $V$ is a $k$-algebra, then $[v,w]\coloneqq vw-wv$ defines a Lie bracket on $V$, so that $V$ is a Lie algebra in a natural way.
		\item This makes $\mathfrak{gl}_n(\mathbb{R} )$ and $\mathfrak{gl}_n(\mathbb{C} )$ into Lie algebras. In fact, the sets listed in exercise 6.1.2 are all Lie algebras, with respect to a Lie bracket induced from $\mathfrak{gl}$.
		\item $\mathfrak{su} (2)$ and $\mathfrak{so}_3(\mathbb{R} )$ are isomorphic as Lie algebras over $\mathbb{R} $.
	\end{itemize}
\end{exercise}
\begin{solution}
	Let $k$-$\mathsf{Lie} $ be the category of Lie algebras over a field $k$. Objects in $k$-$\mathsf{Lie} $ are clearly $k$-Lie algebras. Morphisms in $k$-$\mathsf{Lie} $ are $k$-linear maps $\phi : \mathfrak{u}  \to \mathfrak{v} $ such that
	\[
		\phi \left( [u,v]_{\mathfrak{u} }  \right) =[\phi (u),\phi (v)]_{\mathfrak{v} } 
	.\]
	This is a fairly natural request. Note that the identity map satisfies this requirement:
	\[
		1([u,v])=[u,v]=[1(u),1(v)]
	.\]
	Additionally, for $\phi : \mathfrak{u}  \to \mathfrak{v} $, $\psi : \mathfrak{v}  \to \mathfrak{w} $ morphisms in $k$-$\mathsf{Lie} $, we have
	\[
		(\psi \phi )([u,v]_{\mathfrak{u} } )=\psi \left( [\phi (u),\phi (v)]_{\mathfrak{v} }  \right) =\left[ (\psi \phi )(u),(\psi \phi )(v) \right]_{\mathfrak{w} } 
	.\]
	Therefore, $\psi \phi : \mathfrak{u}  \to \mathfrak{w} $ is a $k$-$\mathsf{Lie} $ morphism, and $k$-$\mathsf{Lie} $ is a category.

	Note that in any lie algebra,
	\begin{align*}
		[u,v]+[v,u]&=[u,v]+0+[v,u]+0\\
			   &=[u,v]+[u,u]+[v,u]+[v,v]\\
			   &=[u,v+u]+[v,u+v]\\
			   &=[u,u+v]+[v,u+v]\\
			   &=[u+v,u+v]\\
			   &=0
	.\end{align*}
	Therefore, $[u,v]=-[v,u]$.

	Clearly, the commutator is a Lie bracket:
	\[
		[u,u]=u^2-u^2=0
	;\]
	\begin{align*}
		[au+bv,w]&=(au+bv)w-w(au+bv)\\
			 &=auw+bvw-awu-bwv\\
			 &=auw-awu+bvw-bwv\\
			 &=a[u,w]+b[v,w]
	;\end{align*}
	\[
		[[u,v],w]+[[v,w],u]+[[w,u],v]
	\]
	\[
		=(uv-vu)w-w(uv-vu)+(vw-wv)u-u(vw-wv)+(wu-uw)v-v(wu-uw)
	\]
	\[
		=uvw-vuw-wuv+wvu+vwu-wvu-uvw+uwv+wuv-uwv-vwu+vuw
	\]
	\[
		=0
	.\]
	I believe it is a Lie bracket in the \emph{natural way} because all $\Alg{k}$ morphisms $\phi : V \to W$ are also Lie algebra morphisms, when $V$ and $W$ are endowed with the commutator:
	\[
		\phi ([u,v])=\phi (uv-vu)=\phi (u)\phi (v)-\phi (v)\phi (u)=[\phi (u),\phi (v)]
	.\]
	
	I think I can skip the next bullet point, since we have shown enough to show these sets are $\mathbb{R} $-algebras or $\mathbb{C} $-algebras, and this holds for all $k$-algebras.

	We have already that $\mathfrak{so}_3(\mathbb{R} )\cong \mathfrak{su} (2)$ as $\mathbb{R} $-vector spaces, so we can simply show they are isomorphic as Lie algebras. Will complete after previous problem.
\end{solution}

\begin{exercise}
	Let $M$ be a module over a ring $R$. A \emph{finite composition series} for $M$ is a decreasing series of submodules
	\[
		\left\langle 0 \right\rangle =M_n \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq M_0 = M
	,\]
	in which all quotients $M_i /M_{i+1} $ are \emph{simple} $R$-modules (exercise 3.5.4). The length of the series is the number of strict inclusions, and the composition factors are the quotients $M_i/M_{i+1} $.

	Prove a Jordan-H\"older theorem for modules: any two finite composition series of a module have the same length and the same multiset of composition factors.

	We say $M$ has \emph{length} $m$ if $M$ admits a finite composition series of length $m$. This notion is well-defined as a consequence of this proof.
\end{exercise}
