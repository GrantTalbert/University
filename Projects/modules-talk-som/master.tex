\begin{abstract}
	These are notes for a talk given to the \href{https://societyofmathematics.github.io/}{Society of Mathematics} in the Fall 2026 semester, as part of their semesterly Colloquium. This semester's topic is on symmetries and invariants. In section 1, we will motivate modules by discussing how the structure of an abelian group is precisely that of a $\mathbb{Z} $-module. In section 2, we will introduce rings, and modules over a ring. In section 3, we introduce standard linear algebra constructions, such as $R$-linear maps, kernels, images, and products. We will also discuss how a nontrivial torsion submodule obstructs the existence of a basis for a module over an arbitrary ring, which will motivate quotients as a way of correcting this issue. We will conclude in section 4 with an introduction to chain complexes and homology, since chain complexes are a powerful modern tool for producing computable and interesting invariants from abstract objects.
\end{abstract}
